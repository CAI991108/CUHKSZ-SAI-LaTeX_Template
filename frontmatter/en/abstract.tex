\begin{abstract}
Reviewing documents for a course or a small organisation is slow: the decision needs
both scalable extraction and accountable judgement. We present a human-in-the-loop
document triage assistant. A classifier scores each incoming document, a claim
extractor proposes the evidence behind the score, and low-confidence items are routed
to a human reviewer who accepts, edits or rejects the proposal. The prototype is
reproducible from a pinned environment and a small synthetic corpus. On 48 labelled
items, the assistant reduced the median review time from 6.5 to 3.8 minutes, but 9 of
48 items required a reviewer override, mostly when a document mixed two topics. We
report the failure cases, the audit trail and the limits of the evaluation. The
contribution is not a better classifier; it is a workflow that keeps the human
decision and its evidence inspectable.
\end{abstract}
